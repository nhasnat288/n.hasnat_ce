## Research in Brief

This research project explores a sustainable approach to geotechnical engineering by investigating the partial replacement of natural sand with **crushed waste glass (CWG)** — both as a standalone additive and in combination with **fly ash (FA)** — for applications in pavement subgrade and other geotechnical uses.

This was my first research project and provided me with my first formal experience in conducting structured research and analysis.

The growing demand for natural sand in construction has led to environmental degradation and resource depletion. Simultaneously, waste glass poses a significant disposal challenge worldwide. This project addresses both problems by evaluating whether fine-grained crushed waste glass can serve as a viable substitute for natural sand in geotechnical applications.

The study was conducted in two phases:

1. **Phase I — CWG alone:** Investigating the geotechnical properties (compaction, CBR, shear strength, permeability) of sand–glass mixtures at varying replacement ratios (0%, 10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50%).

2. **Phase II — CWG + Fly Ash stabilization:** Optimizing the combined effect of crushed waste glass and fly ash to enhance the mechanical performance of the sand–glass composite for pavement applications. Fly ash was introduced at varying percentages (5%, 10%, 15%) with an optimized glass content to improve strength and reduce swelling.

**Research Period:** June 2022 – August 2024 &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Affiliation:** Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, SUST, Bangladesh

---

## Key Findings

- Partial replacement of sand with CWG at **10–20% by weight** showed improved or comparable compaction characteristics and shear strength relative to natural sand alone.
- At higher replacement levels (>30%), the shear strength decreased, indicating diminishing returns beyond a threshold.
- The addition of **fly ash** at an optimal percentage significantly enhanced the **California Bearing Ratio (CBR)** and **unconfined compressive strength (UCS)** of sand–CWG mixtures, making them suitable for pavement subgrade applications.
- The optimized blend demonstrated reduced swell potential, improved load-bearing capacity, and enhanced durability under soaked conditions.
- The findings confirm that CWG + FA stabilization is a **cost-effective and environmentally sustainable** alternative to conventional pavement subgrade materials.
